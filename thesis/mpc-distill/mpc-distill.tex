\chapter{Model-Predictive Control and MPC Distillation}
\label{chap:mpc-distill}

\section{Motivation: evaluate with the WM, do not train on it}
\label{sec:mpc-motivation}

Chapter~\ref{chap:offline-online} showed that every attempt to use the
LeWM as a source of \emph{training} signal degrades the offline policy.
Compounding latent error corrupts Bellman targets
(\S\ref{sec:oo-exp1}), value-aware joint training teaches the WM to
hallucinate high-$Q$ states (\S\ref{sec:oo-exp3}), uncertainty
penalties either fail to discriminate or actively punish task-solving
actions (\S\ref{sec:oo-exp45}), and analytic policy gradients through
the WM are too noisy to optimise (\S\ref{sec:oo-exp8}). All four
failure modes share a structural feature: they require the WM to play
the role of an environment or a critic during training.

The same chapter does \emph{not} undermine a much weaker claim: that the
WM, applied for one or two steps starting from a real on-policy latent,
gives a noisy-but-informative \emph{score} for the initial action chunk
when that score is read out through the learned $Q$-function. We never
have to trust the predicted next-state as ground truth; we only have to
trust the ordering it induces over a small set of candidate action
chunks. This chapter takes exactly that view. Training stays fully
offline on real data, so the critic remains honest. At inference time
we expand the action search using the WM as a rollout-and-score oracle.
Because the inference-time procedure produces actions that
systematically beat what the actor proposes on its own, we then close
the loop by distilling those actions back into the policy during
continued offline training---still without feeding any WM-generated
transition to the critic.

Formally, at latent state $z_t$ we select an action chunk
\begin{equation}
a_t^\star \;=\; \arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}_t(z_t)}\; J(a;\,z_t,\,f_\psi,\,Q_\theta),
\label{eq:mpc-formal-problem}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_t(z_t)$ is a small candidate set drawn from a
learned proposal distribution and $J$ is a score function built from a
short WM rollout and the learned $Q$. The remainder of the chapter
specifies $\mathcal{A}_t$, $J$, and the optional refinement procedure
that turns Eq.~\eqref{eq:mpc-formal-problem} into a usable test-time
policy (\S\S\ref{sec:mpc-bon}--\ref{sec:mpc-fmq}); discusses two ways
to distil the resulting $a^\star$ back into the actor
(\S\S\ref{sec:mpc-offline-distill}--\ref{sec:mpc-interleaved}); and
collects the headline results (\S\ref{sec:mpc-results}).

\paragraph{Relation to classical model-based offline RL.}
Methods such as MOPO~\cite{yu2020mopomodelbasedofflinepolicy} and
COMBO~\cite{yu2021combo} use a learned dynamics model directly in the
TD target, with an additive pessimism penalty derived from model
uncertainty. Our position is empirical: even with the same uncertainty
penalties tested in \S\ref{sec:oo-exp45}, that approach collapses on
this benchmark. Inference-time use of the WM is by construction more
forgiving---a wrong predicted next-state moves an action up or down a
small ranked list of candidates rather than poisoning the Bellman
target a critic is supervised against.

\section{Best-of-$N$ MPC at inference}
\label{sec:mpc-bon}

The simplest scheme samples $N$ action chunks from the learned actor,
rolls each forward $H$ steps in the WM with on-policy continuation
actions, scores each rollout, and returns the first action chunk of the
best candidate. This is the standard sampling-MPC template with two
specialisations: the proposal distribution is the trained
flow-matching actor of \S\ref{sec:hiql-flow}, not a hand-tuned
Gaussian, and the score is computed in the latent space of the LeWM.

\paragraph{Proposal sampling.} At decision time $t$ with current latent
$z_t$, draw $N$ noise vectors $\{x_0^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N \sim
\mathcal{N}(0, I_{25})$ and integrate the BC-flow head of the actor
$\pi_\theta$ with $S=10$ Euler steps to obtain $N$ candidate action
chunks:
\begin{equation}
a_0^{(i)} \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\big(\Phi_\theta(z_t, x_0^{(i)}),\,-1,\,1\big),
\qquad i = 1,\dots,N,
\label{eq:mpc-proposal}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_\theta$ denotes the flow integrator of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:flow-euler-clean}.

\paragraph{Rollout with continuation re-sampling.} Each candidate is
rolled forward $H$ WM steps. After every WM step we re-sample the next
action from the actor at the predicted state---an unbiased estimate of
the continuation policy rather than a fixed open-loop plan:
\begin{align}
z_{h+1}^{(i)} &= f_\psi\!\big(z_h^{(i)},\,a_h^{(i)}\big), \nonumber\\
a_{h+1}^{(i)} &= \mathrm{clip}\!\big(\Phi_\theta(z_{h+1}^{(i)},\,\tilde x_0^{(i,h)}),\,-1,\,1\big),
\qquad \tilde x_0^{(i,h)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{25}),
\label{eq:mpc-rollout}
\end{align}
for $h=0,\dots,H-1$ and starting from $z_0^{(i)}=z_t$.

\paragraph{Scoring functions.} Three score functions are used in this
chapter:
\begin{align}
J_{\text{Q-term}}(a^{(i)}) &= Q_\theta\!\big(z_H^{(i)},\,a_H^{(i)}\big),
\label{eq:mpc-score-qterm}\\
J_{\text{Q-all}}(a^{(i)})  &= \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\gamma^h\,
   Q_\theta\!\big(z_{h+1}^{(i)},\,a_{h+1}^{(i)}\big),
\label{eq:mpc-score-qall}\\
J_{\text{dense+Q}}(a^{(i)})&= \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\gamma^h\,
   \tilde r^{\text{dense}}\!\big(z_{h+1}^{(i)}\big)
   \;+\;\gamma^H\,Q_\theta\!\big(z_H^{(i)},\,a_H^{(i)}\big),
\label{eq:mpc-score-densepq}
\end{align}
with $\tilde r^{\text{dense}}(z) = -\|z-z_g\|_2 / s$ ($s=10$,
cf.~\S\ref{sec:oo-setup}). When there is more than one critic head
($\mathrm{num\_qs}=2$ in our agent), $Q_\theta$ above denotes the
ensemble mean. The chosen action is then
\begin{equation}
a_t^\star \;=\; a_0^{(i^\star)},
\qquad i^\star = \arg\max_{i}\; J\!\big(a^{(i)}\big),
\label{eq:mpc-argmax}
\end{equation}
and the agent commits the $25$-dimensional chunk $a_t^\star$ to the
real environment, dispatched as $5$ consecutive $5$-D sub-actions.

The full BoN-MPC procedure is summarised as Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc-bon}
(corresponding to \texttt{eval\_mpc.py}, lines~$205$--$222$).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Best-of-$N$ MPC ($K_{\text{grad}}=0$ branch of
   \texttt{\_make\_mpc\_fn}, \texttt{eval\_mpc.py})}
\label{alg:mpc-bon}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require current latent $z_t$, actor $\pi_\theta$, critic $Q_\theta$,
         WM $f_\psi$, goal latent $z_g$, hyperparameters $N$, $H$,
         $\gamma$, $s$, scoring $J$.
\State Tile $z_t$ to a batch $z_{\text{batch}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 192}$.
\State Sample $\{x_0^{(i)}\}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{25})$,
       set $a_0^{(i)}\leftarrow\Phi_\theta(z_{\text{batch}},x_0^{(i)})$
       (Eq.~\eqref{eq:mpc-proposal}).
\State $z_t \leftarrow z_{\text{batch}}$, $a_t \leftarrow a_0$, $s_t \leftarrow 0$.
\For{$h = 0,\dots,H-1$}
   \State $z_{h+1}\leftarrow f_\psi(z_h, a_h)$.
   \If{$J = J_{\text{Q-all}}$}
      \State $a_{h+1}\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid z_{h+1})$;
             $s_t \mathrel{+}= \gamma^h Q_\theta(z_{h+1}, a_{h+1})$.
   \Else
      \If{$J = J_{\text{dense+Q}}$}
         \State $s_t \mathrel{+}= \gamma^h\,\tilde r^{\text{dense}}(z_{h+1})$.
      \EndIf
      \State $a_{h+1}\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid z_{h+1})$.
   \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$J \ne J_{\text{Q-all}}$} $s_t \mathrel{+}= Q_\theta(z_H, a_H)$. \EndIf
\State $i^\star \leftarrow \arg\max_i s_t^{(i)}$.
\State \Return $a_0^{(i^\star)}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Headline result.} On the $81.6\%$ offline checkpoint
\texttt{sd55445}, the best BoN-MPC configuration ($N=32$, $H=1$,
$J_{\text{Q-term}}$) reaches $88.0\%$ real-env success---a $+6.4\%$
absolute lift purely from inference-time search. The full sweep is in
Table~\ref{tab:mpc-frozen} in \S\ref{sec:mpc-results}. Two
observations: $H=1$ with $J_{\text{Q-term}}$ beats both dense reward
shaping and longer horizons ($H=2,3$ at $K=0$ score $83$--$88\%$
depending on choices), and a separate run with a VAML-finetuned WM
\cite{farahmand2017value} performs no better than the standard FT WM,
confirming that the WM accuracy is not the bottleneck.

\section{Gradient-refined MPC}
\label{sec:mpc-grad}

BoN-MPC is restricted to the actions the proposal distribution actually
samples. Because the WM rollout in \S\ref{sec:mpc-bon} is fully
differentiable, we can refine the first chunk of the winning candidate
by gradient ascent through the score itself.

\paragraph{Grad-MPC update.} Define the differentiable scalarised score
as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mpc-score-qterm}--\eqref{eq:mpc-score-densepq} but
treating the continuation noise samples
$\{\tilde x_0^{(i,h)}\}$ as constants (so the gradient flows only
through the initial chunk $a_0$ and the chained $f_\psi$ and $Q_\theta$
calls). In code (\texttt{eval\_mpc.py}, lines~$153$--$181$) the
continuation actions are wrapped in \texttt{jax.lax.stop\_gradient} so
that the gradient does \emph{not} propagate through the actor's flow
integrator. We then run $K$ raw gradient-ascent steps per proposal:
\begin{equation}
a_0 \;\leftarrow\; \mathrm{clip}\!\big(a_0 + \eta\,\nabla_{a_0} J(a_0;\,z_t),\;-1,\;1\big),
\quad k = 1,\dots,K,
\label{eq:mpc-grad}
\end{equation}
with $\eta = 0.01$. After the $K$ steps, fresh continuation noise is
drawn and a final scoring pass selects the best refined proposal.

\paragraph{Headline result.} With $H=2$, $K=5$, $N=32$,
$J_{\text{Q-term}}$, Grad-MPC reaches $90.0\%$---a further $+2.0\%$
over BoN-MPC and now within $2.8\%$ of the IMPALA baseline. See
Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc-grad} for the full procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Grad-MPC and FMQ
   (\texttt{eval\_mpc.py}, lines~$135$--$202$). The two methods share
   the proposal step and differ only in the inner update on $a_0$.}
\label{alg:mpc-grad}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $z_t$, $\pi_\theta$, $Q_\theta$, $f_\psi$, $z_g$,
         scoring $J$; method-specific HPs $(K,\eta)$ for Grad-MPC
         or $\eta$ for FMQ.
\State Sample $\{x_0^{(i)}\}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{25})$;
       set $a_0^{(i)}\leftarrow\Phi_\theta(z_t, x_0^{(i)})$.
\State Pre-sample continuation noises
       $\{\tilde x_0^{(i,h)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{25})\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$;
       these are constants in the gradient computation below.
\If{Grad-MPC}
   \For{$k = 1,\dots,K$}
      \State $g\leftarrow\nabla_{a_0}\,J(a_0;z_t,\{\tilde x_0\})$
             (BPTT through $H$ WM steps).
      \State $a_0\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(a_0 + \eta\,g,\,-1,1)$.
   \EndFor
\ElsIf{FMQ}
   \State $g\leftarrow\nabla_{a_0}\,J(a_0;z_t,\{\tilde x_0\})$
          (single BPTT pass).
   \State $a_0\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}\big(a_0 + \eta\,g / \|g\|_2,\,-1,1\big)$.
\EndIf
\State Draw fresh continuation noises and re-score
       $a_0^{(1)},\dots,a_0^{(N)}$ as in
       Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc-bon}.
\State \Return $a_0^{(i^\star)}$ with
       $i^\star=\arg\max_i\,s_t^{(i)}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Flow-Matching $Q$ (FMQ): a single normalised step}
\label{sec:mpc-fmq}

Grad-MPC is sensitive to the step size $\eta$ and to the choice of
$K$; large $K$ overshoots, small $K$ underuses the gradient. FMQ
replaces the raw multi-step ascent with a single normalised step under
a trust-region radius $\eta$:
\begin{equation}
a_0^\star \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\left(
   a_0 \;+\; \eta\;\frac{\nabla_{a_0} J(a_0;\,z_t)}{\|\nabla_{a_0} J(a_0;\,z_t)\|_2},\;
   -1,\;1\right).
\label{eq:mpc-fmq}
\end{equation}
The gradient still flows through the full $H$-step WM rollout via BPTT.
We pair FMQ with the multi-step scoring $J_{\text{Q-all}}$ rather than
$J_{\text{Q-term}}$ so that the gradient sums coherent signals from
$Q$ at every intermediate WM state, not just the terminal one.

\paragraph{Sensitivity on the base policy.} Applied to the
$81.6\%$ frozen base policy, FMQ reaches $90.0\%$ at $\eta=0.1$ with
$J_{\text{Q-all}}$---the same peak as Grad-MPC ($K=5$). Performance
collapses sharply above $\eta=0.1$: $\eta=0.3$ already drops to
$57.2\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:mpc-fmq-basepolicy} in
\S\ref{sec:mpc-results}). The base critic is well-calibrated only for
actions in a narrow neighbourhood of what the BC-flow actor would
sample; large normalised steps move actions outside that
neighbourhood, the critic returns unreliable values, and the score
ordering collapses. This sensitivity is the empirical motivation for
\S\ref{sec:mpc-interleaved}: if we train the actor and critic with
labels generated by FMQ, the critic learns to be reliable on
FMQ-shaped actions.

\section{Offline MPC distillation}
\label{sec:mpc-offline-distill}

The simplest way to amortise inference-time MPC into the policy is a
two-phase pipeline: relabel the offline dataset with MPC-selected
actions, then continue training the BC-flow actor on those relabels.
This is the approach in \texttt{offline\_mpc\_distill.py}.

\paragraph{Phase A — relabel.} For every latent observation
$z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}$ in the offline cache we run the
Grad-MPC procedure of \S\ref{sec:mpc-grad} ($N=32$, $H=2$, $K=5$,
$\eta=0.01$, $J_{\text{Q-term}}$) using the offline-trained actor and
critic and the fully fine-tuned WM as the planner components, and
record the resulting $25$-D chunk as $a^{\text{mpc}}(z)$. The output is
a new dataset of $(z,\,a^{\text{mpc}}(z))$ pairs.

\paragraph{Phase B — distil.} We continue training \emph{only} the
BC-flow actor on the relabelled pairs, with the critic and one-step
distillation heads frozen. The loss is the flow-matching BC loss of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:oo-bc} with $a$ replaced by $a^{\text{mpc}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{distil}}(\theta) \;=\;
   \mathbb{E}_{z,\,t\sim U[0,1],\,x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}\!\left[
   \big\|u_\theta\!\big(z,\,x_t,\,t\big) \;-\; \big(a^{\text{mpc}}(z) - x_0\big)\big\|_2^2\right],
\label{eq:mpc-distil-loss}
\end{equation}
with $x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t\,a^{\text{mpc}}(z)$. The optimiser is AdamW
with learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size
$256$, for $10^5$ gradient steps.

\paragraph{Phase C — evaluate.} The resulting actor can be evaluated
standalone (BoN $N=4$ under $Q_\theta$, no WM at test time) or used as
the proposal distribution inside any of
\S\S\ref{sec:mpc-bon}--\ref{sec:mpc-fmq} at test time.

\paragraph{Findings and limitations.} Offline distillation lifts the
\emph{standalone} success rate only modestly (to $\sim 81$--$84\%$,
within the bounds of the offline baseline) but improves the MPC-eval
score significantly. The bottleneck is the critic: it was trained on
the unmodified offline action distribution and remains so. The next
section addresses that by letting actor, critic, and MPC labels
co-evolve during continued offline training.

\section{Interleaved MPC distillation}
\label{sec:mpc-interleaved}

We now turn to the main method. Inside the standard offline FQL loop
(\S\ref{sec:oo-setup}) we periodically regenerate MPC labels from the
\emph{current} agent and inject those labels into each training batch.
The critic and actor are both updated at every step; the actor sees
MPC-shaped actions on a fraction of each batch and gradually learns to
propose them; the critic sees the same actions and updates its
$Q$-values to be reliable on them.

\paragraph{MPC label buffer.} A fixed-size circular buffer
$\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}$ of $(z,\,a^{\text{mpc}}(z))$ pairs is
refreshed every $T_{\text{relabel}}$ gradient steps after an initial
warm-up of $T_{\text{warm}}$ steps. On each refresh we sample
$|\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}|$ states uniformly from the offline cache and
relabel them with the current agent's MPC procedure
(\texttt{train\_interleaved\_mpc.py}, lines~$112$--$219$). The label
generator can be Grad-MPC (Eq.~\eqref{eq:mpc-grad}) or FMQ
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:mpc-fmq}); both options are exposed.

\paragraph{Mixed batch injection.} At each training step we draw a
real-data minibatch $B$ from the offline replay and, if
$\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}$ is filled, replace the first $\lfloor pB\rfloor$
rows with samples from the MPC buffer
(\texttt{inject\_mpc\_into\_batch}, lines~$394$--$419$). The replaced
rows carry the same $(z, a^{\text{mpc}}(z))$ values; observations and
the first action slot are overwritten and the validity mask is reset to
$1$. Importantly, the replacement happens to \emph{both} the actor and
critic targets in the same batch: the critic minimises the standard
TD residual of Eq.~\eqref{eq:oo-critic} on $(z, a^{\text{mpc}}, r, z')$,
where $r$ is the offline-relabelled real reward and $z'$ the offline
next-state stored alongside the action---the critic never sees a
WM-imagined state, only the action label that MPC chose. The default
mix ratio is $p = 0.3$.

A ``separate'' mode is also implemented in which the MPC labels are
used only in an additional actor-only gradient step performed every
$50$ main steps, leaving the critic's batch composition untouched.
Our experiments show that the mixed mode wins decisively
(\S\ref{sec:mpc-results}); we conjecture this is because the critic
must \emph{see} the MPC actions to assign them high $Q$, otherwise the
actor is pulled toward labels the critic cannot endorse.

\paragraph{AWR-filtered variant.} An optional advantage filter keeps
only labels that the current critic prefers over the actor's own
proposal. At relabel time we score both actions:
\begin{align}
\hat A(z) &= Q_\theta\!\big(z,\,a^{\text{mpc}}(z)\big) - Q_\theta\!\big(z,\,\pi_\theta(z)\big),
\label{eq:mpc-awr-adv}\\
w(z) &= \exp\!\Big(\mathrm{clip}\big(\hat A(z)/\beta,\,-10,\,5\big)\Big)\,\mathbb{1}\!\big[\hat A(z) > \tau_{\text{awr}}\big],
\label{eq:mpc-awr-weight}
\end{align}
with temperature $\beta=1$ and threshold $\tau_{\text{awr}}=0$. The
actor-only loss in this mode becomes
$\sum_i w_i (u_\theta(\cdot) - (a_i^{\text{mpc}} - x_0))^2 / \sum_i w_i$.
Empirically (Table~\ref{tab:mpc-interleaved}) AWR filtering does not
beat plain mixed injection on this task; we report it for completeness
and use the plain mixed variant as the default.

The full procedure is summarised as Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc-interleaved}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Interleaved MPC distillation
   (\texttt{train\_interleaved\_mpc.py}, lines~$639$--$758$).}
\label{alg:mpc-interleaved}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require offline dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{off}}$, agent
         $(\pi_\theta,Q_\theta)$, frozen WM $f_\psi$, hyperparameters
         $T_{\text{warm}}$, $T_{\text{relabel}}$,
         $|\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}|$, $p$, label generator
         $\mathcal{M}\in\{\text{Grad-MPC},\,\text{FMQ}\}$.
\State $\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}\leftarrow\emptyset$.
\For{$\mathrm{step} = 1,\dots,T_{\text{total}}$}
   \If{$\mathrm{step} \ge T_{\text{warm}}$
       \textbf{and} $\mathrm{step}\bmod T_{\text{relabel}} = 0$}
      \State Sample $|\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}|$ states from
             $\mathcal{D}_{\text{off}}$.
      \State For each $z$, set
             $a^{\text{mpc}}(z)\leftarrow \mathcal{M}(z;\pi_\theta,Q_\theta,f_\psi)$
             (Alg.~\ref{alg:mpc-grad}).
      \State $\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}\leftarrow\{(z,\,a^{\text{mpc}}(z))\}$;
             reset actor-only optimiser state.
   \EndIf
   \State Sample minibatch $B$ from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{off}}$.
   \If{$\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}\ne\emptyset$}
      \State Sample $\lfloor pB\rfloor$ pairs from
             $\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}$ and overwrite the first
             $\lfloor pB\rfloor$ rows of $B$'s observations and actions.
   \EndIf
   \State Update $(\theta,Q_\theta)$ on $B$ with
          $\mathcal{L}_{\text{critic}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{actor}}$
          (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:oo-critic}--\eqref{eq:oo-bc}).
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Grad-MPC labels vs FMQ labels.} The label generator
$\mathcal{M}$ is a free choice. With Grad-MPC labels the procedure
mirrors offline distillation but with a co-evolving critic; we refer to
these runs as D1--D6 in Table~\ref{tab:mpc-interleaved}. The strongest
configuration in this family (D4: $N=32$, $H=2$, $K=5$, mixed, $p=0.3$)
reaches $92.4\%$ on MPC-eval---the first method in this thesis within
$0.4\%$ of the $92.8\%$ IMPALA target. Replacing Grad-MPC labels with
FMQ labels ($\eta_{\text{train}}=0.1$, $H=2$,
$J_{\text{Q-all}}$) produces qualitatively cleaner actions for the
critic to consume: a single normalised step lies closer to actions the
actor can match, and the critic accordingly learns a $Q$ that is well
calibrated for FMQ-shaped actions. The D7d run
($\eta_{\text{train}}=0.1$, $T_{\text{relabel}}=50{,}000$, $p=0.3$,
$|\mathcal{B}_{\text{mpc}}|=2{,}000$) reaches a standalone peak of
$86.8\%$ at $400$k steps and---when evaluated with FMQ inference at the
fine inference radius $\eta=0.02$---hits \textbf{$96.0\%$} (Table~\ref{tab:mpc-fmq-distilled}),
a $+3.2\%$ absolute improvement over the IMPALA baseline.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:mpc-discussion}

\paragraph{Why this works where WM-environment training failed.} The
WM is used only at inference, only for short rollouts of $H\le 2$, and
only as a source of \emph{rankings} between candidate actions. It is
never the source of a Bellman target. The critic stays trained on real
$(z,a,r,z')$ tuples from the offline buffer, so the four failure
mechanisms of \S\ref{sec:oo-synthesis}---compounding error,
$Q$-corruption, termination-threshold pathology, and the model-exploitation
trap---never come into play.

\paragraph{Training/inference symmetry under FMQ.}
Table~\ref{tab:mpc-fmq-basepolicy} and Table~\ref{tab:mpc-fmq-distilled}
form a clean before/after comparison. The base critic peaks under FMQ
at $\eta\approx 0.1$ and collapses above; the FMQ-distilled critic peaks
at $\eta\approx 0.02$--$0.03$ and stays above $94\%$ for the entire
range $[0.01,0.05]$. This shift is empirical evidence that the
$Q$-function calibrates to the action distribution it is supervised on:
training the actor on FMQ-shaped actions implicitly trains the critic
to assign reliable values to those same actions, which then permits
much finer-grained inference-time refinement.

\paragraph{Limitations and natural next steps.} Every experiment in
this chapter is on a single OGBench task; we have not tested cross-task
or cross-environment generalisation. There is no real online
interaction at any point; distillation is offline throughout. The
dominant compute cost is the periodic relabeling pass, which scales
linearly in $N$, $H$, and the buffer size; we have not explored
amortising this across tasks. Finally, the success of the
inference-time procedure depends on the WM being accurate enough over
$1$--$2$ steps; longer planning horizons in the WM remain blocked by
the compounding error of \S\ref{sec:oo-exp1}.
