\chapter{Offline-to-Online with the Latent World Model}
\label{chap:offline-online}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:oo-motivation}

The HIQL pipeline of \S\ref{sec:hiql-lewm} and its WGSP extension
(Chapter~\ref{ch:wgsp}) produce strong \emph{offline} agents in the LeWM
latent space, but their final scores plateau short of the privileged-state
baselines reported in OGBench \cite{ogbench_park2025}. On the
\texttt{visual-cube-single-singletask-task1-v0} benchmark, our best frozen
flow-matching policy reaches a $250$-episode success rate of $81.6\%$
(seed \texttt{sd55445}), while the IMPALA \cite{espeholt2018impala}
baseline reported by OGBench reaches $92.8\%$. A natural next step is
\emph{offline-to-online} fine-tuning: continue training the policy with
fresh interaction beyond the static offline dataset.

In a physical setting, additional online interaction requires either a
real robot or a high-fidelity simulator. We already have neither, but we
\emph{do} have a learned latent dynamics model $f_\psi$
(\S\ref{sec:lewm-bg}). The temptation is immediate: wrap the latent
predictor as a Gymnasium environment, swap it in for the real
\textsc{OGBench} env, and let the agent collect synthetic transitions in
the latent space. This chapter documents the eight experiments we ran
along that line, none of which beat the offline baseline. The synthesis
in \S\ref{sec:oo-synthesis} characterises the four distinct ways the
attempt fails, and motivates the conceptual pivot of
Chapter~\ref{chap:mpc-distill}: keep the WM out of the training loop but
use it at \emph{inference} time.

\section{The latents are sufficient: a positive control}
\label{sec:oo-b2}

Before describing the failures, it is important to rule out the obvious
``maybe the LeJEPA latents simply do not contain enough task information''
hypothesis. We ran a sanity-check experiment, denoted \textbf{B2}, that
isolates the encoder from the predictor.

\paragraph{Setup.} We use the real OGBench environment
\texttt{visual-cube-single-singletask-task1-v0} as the dynamics source
but replace its raw $224{\times}224{\times}3$ pixel observations with the
frozen LeJEPA encoder output. A thin \texttt{gymnasium.ObservationWrapper}
(implemented as \texttt{JEPAObsWrapper} in
\texttt{envs/jepa\_obs\_wrapper.py}) maps each pixel observation
$o_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}$ to the $192$-dimensional latent
$z_t = E_\xi(o_t)$, where $E_\xi$ is the same encoder that drives the
WM (\S\ref{sec:lewm-bg}). The agent is the action-chunked Flow Q-Learning
agent (\texttt{agents/acfql.py}, \S\ref{sec:hiql-flow}) trained with
$5\times 10^5$ offline gradient steps on the JEPA-encoded play dataset
followed by $5\times 10^5$ steps of \emph{real} online interaction
collected by the policy and added to the replay buffer.

\paragraph{Result.} The B2 agent reaches a final per-episode success rate
of $1.00$ after the online phase (run \texttt{qc\_b2\_t1\_s0},
\texttt{sd55223}, $10^6$ training steps).

\paragraph{Implication.} The $192$-dimensional LeJEPA latents carry
enough task-relevant information for an offline-to-online RL agent to
solve the task perfectly when given access to true dynamics. Any
subsequent failure of the WM-environment experiments must therefore be
attributable to the \emph{predictor} $f_\psi$, not to the encoder
$E_\xi$. We refer back to this control throughout the chapter.

\section{Common setup for the WM-environment experiments}
\label{sec:oo-setup}

All experiments in \S\S\ref{sec:oo-exp1}--\ref{sec:oo-exp8} share the
same agent, encoder, and offline data, and differ only in how the WM is
used during the online phase. We collect the shared notation here.

\paragraph{World-model environment.} We wrap the frozen LeWM predictor
$f_\psi$ (cross-ref \S\ref{sec:lewm-bg}) as a Gymnasium env, denoted
$\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{A},f_\psi,\tilde r,\tilde T)$ and
implemented in \texttt{envs/wm\_env.py}. State $z_t\in\mathbb{R}^{192}$
is a latent; the action space is $\mathcal{A}=[-1,1]^{25}$, where each
$25$-dimensional ``chunk'' is the row-major concatenation of $5$
consecutive $5$-D end-effector commands (one WM step thus advances the
latent by $5$ real env steps). Episodes run for at most $\tilde T = 40$
WM steps ($\approx 200$ real env steps).

\paragraph{Reward.} Two reward shapes are used throughout the chapter:
\begin{align}
\tilde r_t^{\text{sparse}} &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,\|z_t-z_g\|_2 < \tau_{\text{done}}\,\right], \\
\tilde r_t^{\text{dense}}  &= -\frac{\|z_t-z_g\|_2}{s}, \qquad s=10.
\end{align}
where $z_g$ is the latent encoding of the goal image returned by
\texttt{env.reset(options=\{render\_goal:True\})}, and $\tau_{\text{done}}$
is a per-experiment hyperparameter discussed below. The same dense
reward is used to \emph{relabel} the offline buffer whenever
$\tilde r^{\text{dense}}$ is used online, so that the critic does not
fit a mixture of two reward functions.

\paragraph{Agent.} We use the action-chunked Flow Q-Learning (FQL) agent
of \S\ref{sec:hiql-flow} in \texttt{distill-ddpg} mode. Its critic and
actor losses (\texttt{agents/acfql.py}, lines~39--191) are
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{critic}} &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[\Big(Q_\theta(z,a) -
   \big(r_T + \gamma^{H_{\text{chunk}}}\,m\,Q_{\bar\theta}(z',a')\big)\Big)^2\right],
\label{eq:oo-critic}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{BC}} &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[\big\|u_\theta(z, x_t, t) - (a - x_0)\big\|_2^2\right],
\quad\text{cf.\ Eq.~\eqref{eq:flow-bc-clean}},
\label{eq:oo-bc}
\end{align}
with $\gamma=0.99$, chunk length $H_{\text{chunk}}=5$, target-network
Polyak rate $\tau=0.005$, and BC coefficient $\alpha=100$ on the
one-step distillation head. We freeze nothing on the agent side; what
varies between experiments is the source of $(z,a,r,z')$ during the
online phase.

\section{Experiment~1: plain WM-environment}
\label{sec:oo-exp1}

The simplest possible setup: replace the real env entirely with
$\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}$, sparse reward, no anchoring. Each WM episode
starts from a uniformly sampled latent in an initial-state pool drawn
from the offline data and runs freely for up to $40$ WM steps with
$\tau_{\text{done}}=14$. The agent (starting from a $61.6\%$ offline
checkpoint trained with the unfinetuned LeJEPA WM) collects synthetic
transitions $(z_t,a_t,\tilde r_t,z_{t+1})$ and updates with the standard
FQL losses of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:oo-critic}--\eqref{eq:oo-bc}.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} Online transitions in the WM env should expand
coverage beyond the offline play distribution, exactly as on-policy
rollouts in the real env do.

\paragraph{Result.} Real-env success rate drops to $20$--$30\%$ across
three seeds. Note the contrast with B2 (\S\ref{sec:oo-b2}): the same
agent, same encoder, and same offline dataset reach $100\%$ when paired
with real dynamics and collapse to a quarter of the offline baseline
when paired with WM dynamics.

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.} The WM is a one-step predictor trained
with a contrastive prediction loss
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred-loss-clean}); per-step prediction errors of
$\sim 8$--$9\%$ in cosine distance compound over $40$ steps so that
late-episode latents drift well outside the offline support. The critic
then fits Bellman targets at these drifted states, the actor minimises
a Q-objective on the corrupted critic, and the resulting policy exploits
hallucinated latent-space shortcuts that have no analogue in the real env.

\section{Experiment~2: anchored restart rollouts}
\label{sec:oo-exp2}

To bound the compounding error of \S\ref{sec:oo-exp1} we restrict WM
rollouts to short segments that are periodically re-grounded against the
offline data. The mechanism is the \emph{anchored restart rollout}
(``Variant B'' in the code, \texttt{envs/wm\_env.py}, lines~$163$--$201$).

\paragraph{Mechanism.} Let
$\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{192}$ be the set of all
latents in the offline cache (one per real env step). Every
$L_b$ WM steps we query the nearest neighbour
$\hat z = \arg\min_{z'\in\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}}\|z_t - z'\|_2$ (using
a GPU-resident cosine search via FAISS, falling back to a torch
matmul). We accept the anchor and snap the rollout state to it iff both
of the following hold:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\langle z_t, \hat z\rangle}{\|z_t\|\,\|\hat z\|} \ge \tau_{\cos},
\qquad
\|z_t - \hat z\|_2 \le \tau_{L_2},
\label{eq:oo-anchor-accept}
\end{equation}
with $\tau_{\cos} = 0.95$ and $\tau_{L_2} = 4.0$. If accepted, the
current branch terminates with \texttt{truncated=True} and the next
\texttt{reset()} starts from $\hat z$; otherwise the rollout is
abandoned and a fresh latent is drawn from the initial-state pool. The
transition tuple written to the replay buffer always uses the WM output
as $z_{t+1}$ (no fake snap edge). Formally, the per-step dynamics with
anchoring are
\begin{equation}
z_{t+1} \;=\; \Pi^{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}}\!\Big(f_\psi(z_t,a_t)\Big),
\quad
\Pi^{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}}(z) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\hat z & \text{if Eq.~\eqref{eq:oo-anchor-accept} holds},\\
z      & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:oo-anchor-proj}
\end{equation}
applied only at end-of-branch boundaries (every $L_b=3$ steps).

\paragraph{Results.} Three variants were run, all from the $61.6\%$
base policy unless otherwise stated:
\begin{itemize}
\item Sparse reward, original LeJEPA WM, $\tau_{\text{done}}=10$:
      $13$--$22\%$ across four seeds.
\item Dense reward, partially fine-tuned WM
      (\texttt{lejepa\_play\_ft}), $\tau_{\text{done}}=15$:
      $8$--$54\%$ across four seeds (mean $\approx 31\%$, high variance).
\item Dense reward, fully fine-tuned WM
      (\texttt{lejepa\_play\_ft\_full}), $\tau_{\text{done}}=15$, from
      the $81.6\%$ baseline: $15.2\%$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.} Anchoring successfully bounds the
compounding error, but the fully fine-tuned WM introduces a new
pathology: it compresses latent distances so that the typical
distance-to-goal is $\sim 11$, which is \emph{below} the
$\tau_{\text{done}}=15$ termination threshold. Every WM episode then
terminates at step~$1$ with the success flag set, producing degenerate
$1$-step training data throughout the entire online phase. The high
variance of the partial-fine-tune dense-reward run is the same effect
in milder form: the threshold and the WM's latent geometry interact in
ways that swing the success-rate signal between near-zero (always
``done'') and near-correct (sometimes done) depending on the seed.

\section{Experiment~3: joint training of the world model and policy}
\label{sec:oo-exp3}

A more ambitious approach: rather than freeze the WM, optimise it jointly
with the policy during the offline phase, so that the WM is encouraged
to produce predictions that are useful for $Q$-learning rather than
merely physically plausible.

\paragraph{Joint loss.} During the offline phase the WM predictor
$f_\psi$ has its own AdamW optimiser and receives gradient from a
two-term loss (\texttt{agents/acfql.py}, lines~$193$--$242$):
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{WM}}(\psi) &= \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}(\psi) + \beta(t)\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{value}}(\psi),
\label{eq:oo-joint}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}(\psi) &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[\,
   \frac{\|f_\psi(z,a) - z'\|_2^2}{\|z'\|_2^2 + \varepsilon}\right],
\label{eq:oo-pred}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{value}}(\psi) &=
   \mathbb{E}\!\left[\Big(Q_{\bar\theta}(z,a) -
      \big(r + \gamma\, m\, Q_{\bar\theta}(f_\psi(z,a), a')\big)\Big)^2\right],
\quad a'\sim\pi_\theta\big(\cdot\,|\,\mathrm{sg}[f_\psi(z,a)]\big).
\label{eq:oo-value}
\end{align}
Here $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ stops the action-sampling gradient from
flowing back into $\psi$, and $Q_{\bar\theta}$ is the target critic so
that the WM does not chase a moving target during early training. The
weight $\beta(t)$ ramps linearly from $0$ to a final value
$\beta_{\max}=0.1$ over the first $\beta_{\text{ramp}}=50{,}000$
gradient steps, giving the critic time to stabilise before the WM
starts to listen to it. The coefficient on the prediction term is
$\alpha=1$.

The actor and critic losses themselves are unchanged from
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:oo-critic}--\eqref{eq:oo-bc}. The joint update is then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{joint}}(\theta,\psi) \;=\;
\mathcal{L}_{\text{critic}}(\theta) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{actor}}(\theta) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{WM}}(\psi),
\end{equation}
with $\theta$ and $\psi$ updated through two separate optimisers in the
same gradient step.

\paragraph{Setup and result.} Joint training is followed by an
anchored, dense-reward, WM-env online phase using the resulting
\emph{joint} WM (\texttt{jointtrained\_sd55488}) and the
\emph{joint} policy ($79.6\%$ offline). Real-env success rate after the
online phase: $9.6\%$.

\paragraph{Diagnostic decomposition.} To localise the failure we ran two
follow-up diagnostics, swapping each of the joint-trained components
back to its base counterpart in the online phase:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll r}
\toprule
Run & Policy used online & WM used online & SR (\%) \\
\midrule
\texttt{diag\_basepolicy\_jointwm}  & base ($81.6\%$) & joint WM &  $7.2$ \\
\texttt{diag\_jointpolicy\_basewm}  & joint ($79.6\%$) & base WM & $30.0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The joint-trained WM destroys performance regardless of which policy it
is paired with; the joint-trained policy performs comparably to the
other failed runs when paired with the base WM. The locus of the failure
is therefore $\psi$, not $\theta$.

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.} The $\mathcal{L}_{\text{value}}$ term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:oo-value} measures the Bellman residual between the
target critic at the \emph{real} next state and the target critic at the
WM-\emph{predicted} next state. Minimising it with respect to $\psi$
gives the WM a strong incentive to produce $z_{\text{pred}}$ that
satisfy the Bellman equation under the current $Q_{\bar\theta}$,
regardless of whether they correspond to plausible real dynamics. In
the absence of any constraint forcing $f_\psi$ to remain a faithful
simulator, the predictor learns to hallucinate high-value states---a
classic instance of the model-exploitation failure mode of
model-based RL.

\section{Experiments~4--5: uncertainty-penalised rewards}
\label{sec:oo-exp45}

A standard remedy for model exploitation is to penalise predicted
transitions by an estimate of model uncertainty, in the spirit of
MOPO~\cite{yu2020mopomodelbasedofflinepolicy} and COMBO. We test two
uncertainty estimators inside the WMEnv pipeline (anchored, dense
reward, fully fine-tuned WM, $\tau_{\text{done}}=15$, base policy
$81.6\%$). The penalised reward is
\begin{equation}
\tilde r'_t \;=\; \tilde r_t \;-\; \lambda\, u(z_{t+1}, a_t),
\label{eq:oo-mopo-reward}
\end{equation}
with the two estimators below substituted for $u$.

\paragraph{Ensemble disagreement.} Three independently seeded copies of
the fully fine-tuned WM, $\{f_\psi^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^3$, are run in parallel.
The per-step uncertainty is the L$_2$ norm of the per-element standard
deviation of their predictions:
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{ens}}(z,a) \;=\; \Big\|\,\sigma_k\!\big[f_\psi^{(k)}(z,a)\big]\,\Big\|_2,
\label{eq:oo-mopo-ens}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda = 0.6$. Result: $21.2\%$ success.

\paragraph{Nearest-neighbour cosine distance.} A single WM, with
uncertainty estimated as the cosine distance from each predicted latent
to its nearest neighbour in the offline anchor pool
$\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}$:
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{nn}}(z') \;=\; 1 \;-\; \max_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\text{off}}}\;
   \frac{\langle z, z'\rangle}{\|z\|\,\|z'\|},
\label{eq:oo-mopo-nn}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda = 0.07$. Result: $2.4\%$ success---worse than the unpenalised
baseline.

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.} Both penalties act as approximations of
``how far this transition is from the offline support'', but the play
dataset is dominated by undirected exploration of the workspace.
Successful task-solving actions push the cube toward the goal and the
latent toward less-visited regions of the dataset. The NN-distance
penalty therefore discriminates \emph{against} precisely the actions we
want the policy to take, inverting the action ranking under the
$Q$-function. The ensemble penalty is less directly catastrophic
because the three WMs share the same play-dataset bias, so disagreement
is a uniform downward offset on rewards rather than a directional
signal, but both runs additionally suffer from the degenerate $1$-step
episodes of \S\ref{sec:oo-exp2}.

\section{Experiment~6: differentiable WM rollouts}
\label{sec:oo-exp8}

Our final attempt avoids treating the WM as an environment at all.
Instead, the frozen WM is used as a \emph{differentiable simulator} and
an analytic policy-gradient term is added to the actor loss.

\paragraph{Rollout loss.} The actor receives an additional term
(\texttt{agents/acfql.py}, lines~$71$--$109$)
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{roll}}(\theta) \;=\;
   -\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}\!\left[\,\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^k\,
   \tilde r^{\text{dense}}\!\big(z_{t+k+1}\big)\right],
\quad
z_{t+k+1} = f_\psi\!\big(z_{t+k},\,\pi_\theta(z_{t+k})\big),
\label{eq:oo-rollout-loss}
\end{equation}
with $H=3$. Because $f_\psi$ and the flow-matching actor $\pi_\theta$
are both differentiable, the gradient flows from the dense reward back
through three WM steps and through the actor's $10$-step Euler
integration into the actor parameters. The total actor loss becomes
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{actor}} + \lambda_{\text{roll}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{roll}}$.

In contrast with \S\ref{sec:oo-exp3}, the WM is here \emph{frozen}: this
mechanism changes the actor using the WM as a fixed simulator, not the
WM using the actor as a source of value signal.

\paragraph{Setup and result.} Two values of $\lambda_{\text{roll}}$ were
tested, $0.1$ and $1.0$, both with anchoring on, dense reward, and the
partially fine-tuned WM, starting from the $61.6\%$ base. Both runs
collapse to $0.0\%$ real-env success.

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.} The gradient through Eq.~\eqref{eq:oo-rollout-loss}
chains the actor's $10$ flow integration steps and three WM steps. Each
WM step introduces $\sim 8\%$ cosine error in the latent, and each flow
step is a noisy estimate of the flow velocity; the combined gradient
landscape no longer reflects the true return. At $\lambda_{\text{roll}}=1$
the rollout term dominates the BC-flow loss and the actor abandons the
behavioural distribution to chase WM rewards; at $\lambda_{\text{roll}}=0.1$
the rollout gradient is too small relative to BC to influence the
actor. Neither tuning works.

\section{Synthesis}
\label{sec:oo-synthesis}

\paragraph{Four failure mechanisms.} The B2 control rules out the
encoder; every failure above is attributable to the WM
\emph{predictor}, but through four distinct routes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Compounding latent-prediction error.}
      Per-step prediction error of $\sim 8\%$ in cosine distance compounds
      over $\sim 40$-step rollouts. The drifted latents take the policy
      and critic outside the offline support
      (\S\ref{sec:oo-exp1},~\S\ref{sec:oo-exp2}).
\item \emph{$Q$-function corruption.}
      WM-only transitions feed Bellman targets that the critic fits;
      since the WM transitions are systematically wrong, the resulting
      $Q$ ranks real-env actions incorrectly. Every online run suffers
      this, regardless of anchoring or pessimism.
\item \emph{Termination-threshold pathology.}
      Fine-tuned WMs compress latent distances. With a fixed
      $\tau_{\text{done}}$, episodes terminate at step~$1$, producing
      useless training signal (\S\ref{sec:oo-exp2},~\S\ref{sec:oo-exp45}).
\item \emph{WM-as-optimisation-target trap.}
      When the WM is updated against a critic-derived loss it learns to
      hallucinate states that satisfy the Bellman equation rather than
      states that correspond to real dynamics (\S\ref{sec:oo-exp3}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The asymmetry that motivates Chapter~\ref{chap:mpc-distill}.}
The four mechanisms above all share a structural feature: they require
the WM to be a \emph{source of supervision} for the policy or critic,
either through TD targets, dense reward, or policy gradients. The WM is
too inaccurate to play that role. But none of the failures challenges
the weaker claim that the WM, applied for one or two steps starting
from a real on-policy latent, gives a noisy but informative
\emph{ranking} of candidate action chunks. We have not used the WM that
way in any experiment in this chapter. Chapter~\ref{chap:mpc-distill}
takes exactly that approach: we leave training fully offline on real
data, so the critic stays honest, and use the WM only at inference time
to expand the action search beyond what the actor would propose on its
own.
