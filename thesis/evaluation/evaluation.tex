\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chap:experiments}

This chapter collects the empirical evidence for the methods of
Chapters~\ref{chap:wm-as-env}--\ref{chap:online-with-wm}. It is
organised to mirror the three story chapters: \S\ref{sec:exp-wm-as-env}
covers the WM-as-environment family (Chapter~\ref{chap:wm-as-env} plus
the offline-to-online WM-env experiments of
Chapter~\ref{chap:online-with-wm}), \S\ref{sec:exp-wm-as-subgoal}
covers HIQL+LeWM, flow-matching, and WGSP, and \S\ref{sec:exp-mpc}
covers the BoN/Grad-MPC/FMQ pipeline. A headline cross-chapter
comparison is given in \S\ref{sec:exp-headline}, and the diagnostic
discussion in \S\ref{sec:exp-discuss} closes the chapter.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\subsection{Environment and datasets}
We use \textsc{OGBench} cube-single-play \cite{ogbench_park2025}, a
five-task object-pushing benchmark with $25$-D continuous actions and
either $64{\times}64$ or $224{\times}224$ RGB observations. Each
evaluation runs the five predefined tasks
(\texttt{horizontal}, \texttt{vertical1}, \texttt{vertical2},
\texttt{diagonal1}, \texttt{diagonal2}) for $50$ episodes each, with
a maximum of $200$ steps per episode. The offline-to-online experiments
of Chapter~\ref{chap:online-with-wm} use the single-task variant
\texttt{visual-cube-single-singletask-task1-v0} ($250$ episodes per
evaluation) so that the success rates can be compared against the
OGBench IMPALA reference number ($92.8\%$). World-model and
offline-cache training data come from the expert trajectories
distributed with OGBench.

\subsection{Evaluation protocols}

\paragraph{Native OGBench.}
The agent is rolled out in the real simulator and a task counts as
solved when the cube ends within $4$\,cm of the target. This is the
headline metric for almost every table in this chapter.

\paragraph{Latent goal-reaching.}
An in-distribution sanity check in which goals are sampled from
training data and the agent must drive the latent state to within
$\delta_d$ of the goal embedding in $\mathcal{Z}$. During SAC+HER
training the agent never touches MuJoCo: transitions are produced
entirely by the LeWM predictor inside a latent environment, and a
``success'' is logged when the predicted latent gets within $\delta_d$
of the latent target. We emphasise that this measures only whether the
actor can drive the predictor to a target latent and not whether the
cube task is solved; it must not be compared head-to-head with native
OGBench success rates, and we use it only as a training-time diagnostic.

\paragraph{CEM/MPC planning.}
As an alternative to learning a policy, we replace the actor with a
cross-entropy-method planner that optimises action sequences against
the LeWM rollout. Reported separately in \S\ref{sec:exp-cem-baseline}.

\paragraph{Singletask offline-to-online.}
The Chapter~\ref{chap:online-with-wm} experiments run $5\!\times\!10^5$
offline steps followed by a post-offline phase (either MPC at
inference, interleaved distillation, or one of the failed WM-env
variants); native task success is evaluated over $250$ episodes from
randomised initial states.

\section{Using the World Model as an Environment}
\label{sec:exp-wm-as-env}

This section reports the results for Chapter~\ref{chap:wm-as-env} (the
goal-conditioned WM-as-env experiments) and the offline-to-online
analogues of Chapter~\ref{chap:online-with-wm}
(§\ref{sec:owm-exp1}--§\ref{sec:owm-exp8}). The unified picture is
that no method that uses the WM as an environment substitute beats
its corresponding non-WM baseline; latent-reach rates saturate but
native task success collapses.

\subsection{Curriculum SAC and HER+SAC}
\label{sec:exp-her-results}

The flat curriculum SAC (\S\ref{sec:curr-sac}) advances quickly
through the early curriculum stages but stalls at the longer-horizon
ones; native task success remains in the low single digits across all
runs. HER+SAC (\S\ref{sec:her-sac}) raises the latent-reach metric
substantially --- the agent can drive the predictor to a target latent
in nearly every configuration --- but the same checkpoints collapse to
$0$--$2\%$ native success across all task-sampling modes (curriculum,
fixed-gap, pure-distance) with sparse reward.

\subsection{Joint Hierarchical SAC}
\label{sec:exp-joint-results}

The hierarchical joint SAC formulation (\S\ref{sec:joint}) sweeps the
high-level gap $G$ at two AWR temperatures $\beta\in\{3.0, 20.0\}$. The
best native result is $3.6\%$ at small gap (Table~\ref{tab:joint-gap}),
which is an order of magnitude below the state-observation upper
bound. The performance cliff between $G{=}2$ and $G{=}5$ is sharp and
consistent with the predictor-drift curve of \S\ref{sec:wm-drift}: a
waypoint at $G{=}5$ lies $25$ raw frames into the future and is past
the predictor's reliable horizon.

To isolate the encoder's role we re-implemented the same two-level
architecture directly on $28$-D state observations
(\texttt{train\_joint\_state.py}): both $G{=}5$ and $G{=}10$ score
$0.0\%$ on all five native tasks. The failure mode is distinct from
the flat state baseline (which solves three tasks): an unreliable
high-level gives the low-level a noisier effective goal than a direct
physical goal, triggering premature entropy collapse. This indicates
the hierarchical decomposition itself is fragile without a reliable
high-level policy, independently of representation quality.

transitions into the replay buffer during an online phase run entirely
inside the world model. On the single task
(Table~\ref{tab:mpc-online-e2}), freezing the critic is decisive: with
the critic updated on the imagined transitions the policy collapses
(E2a, $30.7\%$), whereas freezing it lifts the standalone policy from
the $76.4\%$ offline start to $90.7 \pm 0.8\%$ final and
$94.8 \pm 0.7\%$ peak (E2c), with no planner at test time. Applying the
FMQ-MPC planner on top of the best E2c checkpoints does not improve on
this ($93.2 \pm 0.7\%$), because the actor has already absorbed the
controller. The goal-conditioned extension
(Table~\ref{tab:mpc-online-e3}) does not beat its $90.0\%$ offline
baseline on final success; the leakage-free variants are strongest at
$\approx 84\%$.

\section{Diagnostics and Failure-Mode Analysis}
\label{sec:exp-discuss}

The single most important finding of this chapter is the gap between
the two evaluation regimes. Every method that optimises latent
distance --- SAC+HER ($>\!87\%$ latent-reach), the CEM planner ($72\%$
latent-reach), and the legacy $224$ stack ($>\!95\%$ latent-reach) ---
can drive the predictor to a target embedding almost perfectly, yet
the same policies solve at most a few percent of the native cube task
and the CEM planner solves none. High latent-reach is necessary but
nowhere near sufficient: a latent trajectory that terminates at the
goal embedding need not correspond to a physical trajectory that
places the cube, precisely because of the predictor drift documented
in \S\ref{sec:wm-drift}. Any latent-reach number in this chapter
should be read as a training diagnostic, never as a task result.

The progression of native success rates --- curriculum SAC stalling
near zero, HER+SAC still at zero natively, hierarchical SAC at a
modest $3.6\%$, HIQL+LeWM at $20.8\%$, the offline-to-online MPC
pipeline at $96.0\%$ --- maps directly onto the changing role of the
WM. As long as the WM is in the training loop, performance is
bounded by the predictor's accuracy, and the predictor is not accurate
enough. Once the WM is restricted to short-horizon scoring at
inference time, the same predictor becomes useful. The most
informative single diagnostic remains the predictor-drift curve
(\S\ref{sec:wm-drift}): the methods whose effective horizon exceeds
roughly $10$--$25$ raw frames fall off a cliff that matches the
joint-SAC gap boundary between $G{=}2$ ($10$ frames, $3.6\%$) and
$G{=}5$ ($25$ frames, $0\%$), and the methods that stay below that
horizon succeed.
