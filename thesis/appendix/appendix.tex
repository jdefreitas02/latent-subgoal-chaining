\section{Offline-to-Online Hyperparameter Sweeps}
\label{app:oo-sweeps}

The tables below collect the full hyperparameter sweeps referenced in
Chapter~\ref{chap:mpc-distill}. All entries use the same environment
(\texttt{visual-cube-single-singletask-task1-v0}, $250$ episodes per
evaluation) and the same offline policy starting point
(\texttt{sd55445}, $81.6\%$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Mixed-injection variant standalone progression} (eval every $100$k steps):
$100$k:~$70.4\%$, \textbf{$200$k:~$90.0\%$}, $300$k:~$77.6\%$,
$400$k:~$81.6\%$, $500$k:~$72.8\%$. The $200$k peak was lost due to
missing intermediate checkpoints; this bug was fixed in all subsequent

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Key takeaways.} Buffer size has the largest single
effect: FMQ-distil~E ($\mathrm{buf}=5000$, peak $89.2\%$) versus
FMQ-distil~D ($\mathrm{buf}=500$, peak $82.4\%$) is a $+6.8\%$ absolute lift. A lower
learning rate paired with a stronger mix ratio (FMQ-distil~L: $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-4}$, $\mathrm{mix}=0.5$) achieves the cleanest single peak ($90.0\%$ at $275$k), suggesting reduced oscillation allows the training to maintain the peak longer. The very low learning rate $5\times 10^{-5}$ (FMQ-distil~K) underperforms: too slow even at $300$k.
\end{table}
