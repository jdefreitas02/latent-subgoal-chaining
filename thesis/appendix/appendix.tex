\chapter{Other methods tried --- SAC family}
\label{app:sac-retrospective}

Before the methodology chapters arrived at their present
structure, three goal-conditioned methods were tried directly on
the OGBench cube data with the LeWM predictor in place of the
real environment. They are documented here rather than in a
methodology chapter because (a) all three failed and (b) the
failure mode was not cleanly diagnosed. We include short descriptions here for completeness.

\paragraph{Curriculum SAC.}
A flat goal-conditioned Soft Actor-Critic agent
\cite{haarnoja2018softactorcriticoffpolicymaximum} trained
entirely on transitions produced by the LeWM predictor, with a
curriculum over goal distance: at stage $k$, goals are sampled
from states $G_k$ steps ahead in expert trajectories and rollouts
are limited to $T_{\max,k} = G_k$ steps. The curriculum advances
quickly through the short-horizon stages but stalls at the longer
ones. Native task success in MuJoCo remains in the low single
digits across all configurations.

\paragraph{HER+SAC.}
The same flat SAC agent augmented with Hindsight Experience
Replay \cite{her} and an episodic replay buffer. Relabelled goals
($p_{\text{HER}} = 0.8$) substantially raise the latent-reach
metric --- the agent learns to drive the WM predictor to a target
latent in nearly every configuration, with latent-reach
saturating above $87\%$ for several configurations --- but the
same checkpoints collapse to $0$--$2\%$ native task success on the
five-task cube suite. The latent-reach-vs-native-success gap is
the diagnostic signature shared with the CEM-planning baseline of
Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments}: the WM and the policy together
can satisfy the WM's notion of goal-reaching without solving the
real task.

\paragraph{Hierarchical SAC with expert-guided stabilisation.}
A two-level extension in which a high-level AWR policy proposes
intermediate subgoals every $G$ steps and a SAC low-level reaches
them, with the low-level actor regularised toward an offline
expert dataset. The best native result is $3.6\%$ at $G=1$ or
$G=2$, with a sharp cliff at $G=5$ that aligns with the
predictor-drift horizon of \S\ref{sec:wm-drift}.

The common pattern across all three methods is the latent-reach
saturation without native transfer. The structural reason was
never fully resolved; the methods that work in the body chapters
all avoid the WM-as-environment framing entirely.

\chapter{TD-MPC2 baselines}
\label{app:tdmpc2-baselines}

The TD-MPC2 family \cite{hansen2024tdmpc2} is the closest neighbour
in the model-based-RL literature to the methods of
Chapters~\ref{chap:wm-planner} and~\ref{chap:mpc-distillation}: it
learns a latent dynamics
model jointly with a value function and uses MPPI-style
sampling-based MPC for action selection. We ran two off-the-shelf
TD-MPC2 variants on
\texttt{visual-cube-single-singletask-task1-v0} with default
hyperparameters: TD-MPC2 + IQL (model trained with the IQL
auxiliary objective) and TD-MPC2 + BC-reg (model trained with a
behaviour-cloning regularisation term). Both collapse to $0.0\%$
on the single-task evaluation. This is consistent with the
model-based-RL failure mode discussed in
Chapter~\ref{chap:wm-env}: when a learned latent dynamics
model is in the TD target without an in-distribution gate, the
critic's values on imagined transitions corrupt the policy. The
runs are included here as a check that the difficulty is not
specific to our pipeline.

\chapter{Implementation Notes}
\label{app:impl}

\paragraph{Hardware.} All experiments are performed on a single NVIDIA
A100 GPU. The replay buffers are GPU-resident; for the largest
configuration the flat buffer occupies approximately $1.5\,$GB.

\paragraph{Optimisers.} Adam with learning rate $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$
for all RL networks (actor, critic/value, temperature, goal-rep, flow),
and AdamW with learning rate $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$ for the world model.
Gradient clipping at $1.0$ is applied to every loss.

\paragraph{Target networks.} Polyak averaging with $\tau = 0.005$ is
applied to all critics, values, and the HIQL goal-rep $\varphi$. Target
updates are performed once per gradient step.

\paragraph{Batching and parallelism.} All RL methods use a batch size
of $256$ and $40$ gradient updates per outer iteration (reduced to $20$
in the dense reward setting). World-model roll-outs are vectorised
across $50$ parallel environments.

\paragraph{Constants.} $\gamma = 0.99$ throughout. The done-threshold
$\delta_d = 2.0$ and the dense-reward scale $\kappa = 1.6$ are
calibrated from the median single-step predictor drift in the LeWM
latent space, reported in \S\ref{sec:wm-drift}.

\chapter{Latent Subgoal Planning Ablations}
\label{app:lsp-ablations}

This appendix collects the Phase-2 Latent Subgoal Planning (LSP)
experiments that are referenced from \S\ref{sec:exp-wm-as-subgoal} but
omitted from the main chapter for brevity. All runs start from the same
Phase-1 checkpoint, use the OGBench \texttt{cube-single} benchmark, and
report the native five-task average success rate. Step-0 is the
Phase-1 starting point at the beginning of Phase 2, Peak is the best
saved checkpoint, and Final is the last saved checkpoint. Several runs
were stopped before $500{,}000$ steps once the trend was clear; the
``Last step'' column records where each run ended. These numbers are
produced by the same evaluation pipeline as the main Phase-2 tables
(Tables~\ref{tab:lsp-phase2-awr} and~\ref{tab:lsp-hrf}) and are directly
comparable to them.

\section{Phase-2 design ablations}
\label{app:lsp-design}

Table~\ref{tab:app-lsp-ablations} reports three groups of design
ablations around the Phase-2 update. The first group varies the
low-level treatment in the no-high-level-update control of
\S\ref{sec:exp-phase2-awr}: the reported control freezes the low-level
policy and distils it toward MPC-refined actions, while the two
additional variants instead keep updating the low-level policy. Freezing
the low level and distilling it with MPC actions is the strongest
control ($87\%$ final), whereas updating the low level without an MPC
nudge or with one degrades to $75$--$67\%$.

The second group isolates the effect of freezing the low-level policy
under the world-model AWR update at $\alpha=1.0$. The reported
configuration freezes the low level; allowing it to update in lockstep
with the high level reduces final success further ($54\%$ frozen versus
$44$--$42\%$ updated), confirming that the instability is driven by the
coupled high-/low-level distribution shift rather than by the high-level
update alone. Reducing the high-level update frequency (every $100$
steps rather than every $4$) partially mitigates the collapse
($64\%$ final), consistent with the interpretation that the AWR update
de-sharpens the Phase-1 high-level policy.

The third group replaces the low-level reachability metric used to score
candidate subgoals. The main chapter uses a latent-distance metric
($\lVert z' - w\rVert$); here we instead score reachability with the
value function $V(z',w)$. The value metric is no better than distance
when nothing is updated (both reach $75\%$ final in the frozen control)
and collapses once the high-level policy is updated ($3$--$4\%$ final),
which is why the latent-distance metric is used throughout the main
chapter.

\section{Real-candidate high-level grounding}
\label{app:lsp-real}

Before settling on the AWR formulation of \S\ref{sec:exp-phase2-awr}, we
tried a more direct high-level grounding mode (\texttt{hl\_mode=real}):
the high-level policy is nudged toward an argmax over real-data subgoals
and high-level self-samples scored by the world-model rollout. This mode
suffers from self-consuming flow distillation --- the high-level flow is
repeatedly trained on its own samples --- and collapses far below the
Phase-1 baseline across the entire sweep over the grounding coefficient
$g$ (\texttt{hl\_ground\_coef}), the low-level MPC coefficient
(\texttt{llmpc}), the winner low-level mode, and the candidate-scoring
mode. Table~\ref{tab:app-lsp-real} reports the sweep. The uniform
collapse is what motivated the move to the AWR update, which trains the
high-level policy on real-data subgoals only.
